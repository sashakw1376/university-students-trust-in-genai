# Trust and Transparency in Generative AI: Comparing Perceptions of Commercial and Institutional Platforms in a University Population
Original research studying student perspectives on university generative AI systems, co-written with Sara Waters. This project was conducted from October to December of 2025 as the capstone for the course CIS4930: Security and Privacy for At-Risk Populations at the University of Florida.

## Abstract
Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) and the rise of institutionally managed systems are transforming higher education.
However, little is known about how the university community
perceives the privacy and security of university generative
AI tools, particularly compared to commercial platforms. We
base our decision to evaluate perceptions of privacy and security on the inverse relationship between trust and perceived
risk. We surveyed 57 students, faculty, and staff at the University of Florida (UF) and conducted three supporting interviews
to investigate (1) how these groups use generative AI tools,
(2) how they perceive privacy and security in commercial
versus institutional platforms, namely NaviGator Chat, and
(3) what risks arise from limited transparency regarding data
handling and policies. Our results indicate that the university
community uses generative AI extensively, but behavior and
trust are motivated by peers and assumptions. Students use
NaviGator Chat less often than commercial platforms; yet,
UF’s institutional status elicits a slightly higher level of trust.
We also show that although users attempt to manage their
privacy, their depth of understanding often constrains effective self-protection. These results guide our recommendations
for deploying transparent and ethical generative AI in higher
education.
